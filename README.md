## About the review

**Review question:** Is there an internal migration flow of older adults from non-urban to urban locations in Canada, influenced by health and/or health care access?

The review addressed four research questions:

1. What motivations and incentives for migration do older adults report, and what role does health play among them?
2. What associations between health and migration or residential relocation have been reported?
3. How do the profiles of older movers differ by direction of move (e.g., rural-to-urban vs. urban-to-rural)?
4. How are the key concepts "older adult", "rural", "internal mobility" and "health" defined and measured in the literature?

### Abstract

Existing sources of evidence cite a wide array of motivations for older adults’ migration/residential relocation within Canadian borders, including health-related issues. However, there is limited research on health as a motivation for older adults’ migration/residential relocation. This review explores the role of the internal mobility of older adults in Canada for health-related reasons, with a particular focus on the rural-urban axis. Sources of evidence addressing migration/relocation within Canadian borders – regardless of distance moved – were included. Changes of residence into long-term or assisted living, and seasonal migration, were excluded. Of the 3165 documents screened, 17 were chosen for analysis. The results consistently suggest that older adults with a history of poor health or high healthcare needs are more likely to relocate than their healthier counterparts. Relocation occurs more often towards small or mid-sized urban centres than to large metropolitan areas. While involuntary relocations are likely to produce adverse outcomes, preventing desired moves can also have negative consequences for the health and well-being of involuntary stayers.

### Methods in brief

The review followed the Joanna Briggs Institute (JBI) methodology for scoping reviews and is reported in accordance with the PRISMA extension for scoping reviews (PRISMA-ScR). Searches of Web of Science, Scopus, MEDLINE (PubMed), Elsevier (Engineering Village/GeoRef, Inspec, Compendex) and ProQuest Dissertations & Theses Global were conducted in August 2023 and updated in November 2025, and the reference lists of included sources were checked for additional sources. Screening was carried out in Covidence® (first search) and Rayyan® (updated search). Data were extracted manually, and Claude (Anthropic) was then used to summarise, validate and categorise the extracted data. All records were manually verified by the authors. The prompts used are listed in [`Claude_Prompts.md`](Claude_Prompts.md), and the full search strategy is available in the published protocol.
